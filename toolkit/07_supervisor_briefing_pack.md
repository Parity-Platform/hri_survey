# Supervisor briefing pack

HRIT found that workers mirror their supervisor's stance on robotics. Where supervisors were prepared and communicated openly, worker concerns were lower. This pack prepares supervisors before staff are engaged. Use it in a 60 to 90 minute session with all supervisors of the affected areas.

## 1. What supervisors need to know before staff do

| Topic | Questions to settle with management |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Why is automation considered? Which drivers matter most? |
| Scope | Which areas, lines and tasks are in scope? What is out of scope? |
| Status | What is decided and what is still open? When will open points be decided? |
| People | What commitments exist on jobs, redeployment and retraining? |
| Training | Who will be trained, when, by whom, during paid time? |
| Safety | What is the escalation procedure? Who is accountable for errors? |
| Data | Will robot data be used to assess individuals? (Recommended answer: no, without explicit agreement) |
| Consultation | How will staff be consulted, and how will results be used? |

Supervisors should not be asked to announce a change they cannot explain. If an answer is not available, agree what supervisors say instead (see section 3).

## 2. The supervisor's role in the consultation

- Encourage participation; do not select participants or ask who took part.
- Allow time within the shift for questionnaires, interviews and workshops.
- Pass questions you cannot answer to the coordinator and report back to the team within one week.
- Take part in the supervisor questionnaires and a workshop as a participant, not as a moderator.

## 3. Common questions and suggested answers

| Question from staff | Suggested answer |
|---|---|
| "Will I lose my job?" | "No decision has been taken. The company has committed to [commitment]. If you are worried, say so in the questionnaire or interview: this concern is exactly what the consultation is for." |
| "Why are we doing this?" | "The reasons given by management are [reasons]. If you see other reasons or risks, the consultation is the place to raise them." |
| "Is it safe?" | "Collaborative robots are designed to work near people with speed and force limits. Before any use, there will be training and a clear stop and escalation procedure. Tell us what would make you feel safe." |
| "Will the robot be used to watch us?" | "The company's position is [position]. Ask the coordinator if this is not clear." |
| "Who is to blame if the robot makes a mistake?" | "This will be set out in writing before any deployment. Your input on it is welcome." |
| "I do not have time for this." | "Participation is in paid time. I will organise cover for [slot]." |
| "Nobody will listen anyway." | "Results will be published to everyone by [date], and management will explain how the input was used." |

## 4. Checklist

### Before announcement

- [ ] Briefing session attended
- [ ] Answers to section 1 received from management
- [ ] Agreed wording for open points
- [ ] Time slots for team participation planned

### During consultation

- [ ] Team informed in a briefing, not only by notice
- [ ] Questions logged and forwarded weekly
- [ ] No pressure on individuals to take part or not

### Before go-live

- [ ] Task changes explained to each affected person
- [ ] Training completed before first shared shift
- [ ] Escalation card posted at every robot cell
- [ ] Contact for problems known to every shift

### Three months after go-live

- [ ] Team meeting on experience so far
- [ ] Follow-up survey run (Manual Worker and Operator questionnaires)
- [ ] Adjustments agreed and communicated

## 5. One-page summary for supervisors

1. Your team will follow your lead. Be open, factual and consistent.
2. Do not promise what management has not committed to in writing.
3. Say what is known, what is not known, and when it will be known.
4. Speak about tasks, training and safety, not only about the technology.
5. Make time for your team to take part, and do not monitor who does.
6. Bring questions back within a week, even if the answer is "not decided yet".
