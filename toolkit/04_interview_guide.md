# Semi-structured interview guide

Duration: 30 to 45 minutes. One interviewer, optionally one note taker. Use a quiet room away from the line manager's office, or a phone call.

## Before the interview

- Confirm signed consent, including whether audio recording is allowed.
- Record only the participant code, stakeholder group, department and prior exposure to robots (daily, weekly, rarely, never). No names in notes.
- Explain: no right or wrong answers; the interviewer has no role in performance reviews; the participant can skip any question.

## Core questions (all groups)

1. Describe a normal working day. Which tasks take most of your time and effort?
2. What do you know about collaborative robots? Where did that information come from?
3. If a robot worked next to you or your team, what could improve? What could get worse?
4. What would make you feel safe working near a robot? What would make you feel unsafe?
5. Who should decide whether and how robots are introduced? What role should you have?
6. What information would you need, and from whom, before such a change?
7. What training would you need? In what format: classroom, on the line, with a colleague?
8. If something went wrong with the robot, what should happen? Who should you call?
9. Who should be responsible when a robot or its software makes a mistake?
10. Is there anything else the company should know before deciding?

## Additional questions by group

### Manual workers

- Which of your tasks would you be glad to hand over to a robot? Which would you want to keep?
- How would you like to hear about changes to your tasks: from your supervisor, in a staff meeting, in writing?
- What would a fair retraining offer look like for you?

### Operators and technicians

- How easy is it to understand what the robot will do next? What helps?
- How do you recover from a failure today? What tools or access are missing?
- Does operating the robot make your job more skilled, or more repetitive?

### Supervisors and managers

- What are the main reasons the company is considering automation?
- How prepared is your team in terms of communication, training and coordination?
- What questions do you expect from your team, and which are hardest to answer?
- What support would you need to manage a mixed human and robot team?

### SME clients

- Does the use of robots by a supplier change how you view its quality, reliability or responsibility?
- Would you value evidence that staff were consulted before automation?

### Community participants

- How do you see the effect of automation on local jobs and skills?
- What should local training centres offer?

## Closing

- Summarise two or three key points back to the participant and ask if they are correct.
- Explain when and how results will be published.
- Thank the participant.

## After the interview

- Within 48 hours: transcribe or write up notes; remove names, places and details that could identify the participant.
- Delete the audio file once the transcript is checked.
- Code the transcript against the themes in the synthesis report: benefits, risks, training, trust, job security, responsibility, communication, participation.
